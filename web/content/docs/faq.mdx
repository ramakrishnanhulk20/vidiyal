## Does it place orders?

No. The only write verb anywhere in the codebase is `order` / `place` with `dryRun: true`, and the
SDK answers that with the request rather than sending it. It is built in its own function against
its own config, so the read-only context the rest of the product uses is never the one holding a
write verb. A passing idea ends at the request Bitget would have received, on screen, unsent.

## Is the record real?

The record in the demo bundle is a simulated one: the signed ledger of Kaaval's Claude brain, a
paper-trading agent that runs against live Bitget market data. The prices, the books, the funding
and the news around every trade are real. The fills are not, because no money was at risk.

Everything else is real regardless of which record is loaded: the market rebuilt around each entry
comes from Bitget on the day the review runs, and the same command points at a Bitget account with
a read-only key.

## Why grade process and not profit and loss?

Because profit and loss over five trades, or fifty, is mostly luck, and a number that is mostly
luck cannot tell you what to do differently tomorrow. Vidiyal grades the things that were decided
at the moment of the trade: how far the price had walked from the last regular close when you
entered, how big the order was against your equity and against the visible book, whether the stop
held, what friction ate, and whether the stated thesis and the outcome belonged to the same world.

Profit and loss is on the screen as a fact about the trade. It is never an input to the first four
scores.

## What if the model invents a number?

The draft is thrown away. Every number in a written answer is matched by value against the evidence
table those trades produced, and a number no row holds, or a citation to a trade id the table does
not carry, discards the whole answer in favour of the paragraph the table wrote for itself. The
page says the draft was thrown away and lists the invented numbers. Nothing is quietly corrected.

## Can it review my Bitget account?

That is what it is for. Make a read-only API key, put it in `.env`, and the account reader pulls
your fills, your equity and your balance changes through Agent Hub read verbs. Be told plainly:
that path has not been run against a live key yet, because no read-only key exists for this project
so far. See [review a Bitget account](/docs/guides/review-a-bitget-account).

## What does a letter mean?

The weighted total of the five scores out of 100: A at 85 and above, B at 70, C at 55, D at 40, E
below. The letter is a summary. The finding is the five scores and the evidence line under each, so
two B trades can be a friction problem and a discipline problem and read nothing alike.

## Why are there so few trades in the demo?

Because the record is short, and the desk shows the record rather than padding it. The bundle on
the desk holds 5 round trips over four minutes of a simulated agent's ledger, in RNVDAUSDT,
RVOOUSDT, RMUUSDT and RQQQUSDT. Every screen scales with the record: point the same command at a
longer ledger or at an account with a year of history and the shelf, the patterns and the checklist
grow with it.

## What needs a key?

Only three things, and none of them is the core walk. A read-only Bitget key for an account review.
An Anthropic key for the reasoning score and for the model to write an answer; without it the
evidence table writes the answer itself. A Finnhub key for the earnings and macro calendars;
without it the feed still reads SEC EDGAR and GDELT, which need no key. Every Bitget market read,
the whole review of a ledger, the gate and the dry run work with an empty `.env`.

## How does it use Bitget?

Three ways, and take any of them out and there is no product. The record of a real account comes
through Agent Hub read verbs. The context every grade is built from is Bitget market data: candles,
order books, instruments, funding, tickers. And the checklist ends at the Agent Hub dry run. The
whole idea only makes sense on an exchange that lists tokenized stocks trading around the clock,
which is what makes divergence from the last regular close a thing worth grading at all. The full
table is in [the Bitget verbs](/docs/developers/bitget-calls).

## What is Kaaval?

The sister project: a trading agent that runs against live Bitget market data and writes every
decision, order, fill and equity mark into a signed, hash-chained ledger. Vidiyal reviews that
ledger, and vendors Kaaval's Bitget, news and ledger modules so this repo runs on its own. Kaaval
is one source of record, not a requirement: a Bitget account is the other.
