---
title: "Read a review"
---

A review is one bundle file and five screens over it. Open the desk at `http://localhost:3001` and
read it in this order.

## The shelf

`/` stands every round trip on its spine, coloured by grade, newest first. A spine carries the
ticker, the side, the letter, the total out of 100 and the entry and exit days.

Under the shelf is the sources panel, and it is the part to read first: which record this is
(a simulated Kaaval brain, or a read-only Bitget account), the range, the public key the ledger
was checked against, whether the chain verified, and when the bundle was written. A review that
will not say what it read is not worth reading.

## One trade

`/trades/<round trip id>` leads on the letter, then the five scores, each carrying the line that
produced it. From the bundle on the desk today:

```
entry: price was 0.76 percent from the last regular close
entry: spread was 1.8 bps against a usual 1.8 bps, 1.00 times
entry: 04:21 ET is outside the 09:25 to 09:40 ET re-anchoring window
```

Below the scores are the signed fills, the market rebuilt around the entry (divergence at entry
and exit, the session, the spread, the share of the visible book the order took, the stop), the
headlines the feed found in that window, and the judge's own sentence about the trader's note.
A score with no line behind it is a bug, not a design.

## The patterns

`/patterns` is one card per detector that fired, with the trades that prove it. The detectors that
ran and found nothing are named on the same page, so you can tell a clean record from a detector
that never ran. The bundle on the desk has one card, `fee-bleed`, proved by all 5 trades, out of
9 detectors.

## The checklist

`/checklist` is the item each pattern became, with the test the gate will run against the live
market. One pattern, one item. On this bundle that is `cost-under-20-bps`, out of `fee-bleed`.

Nothing on that list was chosen by hand. It is what the record earned.
